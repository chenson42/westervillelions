## 1.85.0 — 2026-10-01

### Security: framework patch

Next.js is updated from 16.3.4 to 16.3.8, which closes a critical advisory (GHSA-vcvr-r3jv-pc5j, remote
code execution in an image-generation API this site does not use) and two high advisories in a
transitive dependency (brace-expansion, GHSA-6j4f-fj2g-mc7p and GHSA-qhr7-859c-m2p7). No behaviour
change; the full test suite, build and e2e run were repeated on the patched versions before release.

Four changes ship together in this release. Two of them change how the treasurer works day to day
(reimbursements and the public-fund warning), so those sections end with what to confirm.

### Feature: Reimbursements no longer need board approval

**Value:** The board decided that member expense reimbursements should not wait on a board vote.
Until now every request sat in a queue until a board approver acted, then went back to the
treasurer to be paid. The treasurer now reviews and pays each request alone, and the board looks
at what was paid afterward.

#### What's New

- Members submit a reimbursement exactly as before, with a receipt. The request is emailed to the
  treasurer (the holder of the Treasurer board position) instead of to the board. If the treasurer
  cannot act on it, for example because it is their own request, it goes to the other people who
  can record in the Ledger.
- The treasurer reviews the request and either marks it paid, which posts the expense to the
  books, or rejects it with a reason. The member is emailed either way.
- The board no longer approves reimbursements. Their review happens on the **Paid** tab of the
  reimbursements page, which now lists the newest payments first and shows who paid each one, from
  which fund, and on what date. It pages through the full history instead of stopping at the first
  fifty, and it says in a sentence that paid reimbursements are listed there for board review.
- Requests the board had already approved before this release can still be paid or rejected.
- The "Pending Reimbursement" card on the admin home page now appears only to people who can act
  on it.
- Members see their request as "Awaiting Treasurer", and the confirmation after submitting says
  the treasurer will review it.
- The Treasury User's Guide's Reimbursements section describes the new process, and the role
  management screen no longer says the Approve permission covers reimbursements.

#### Safeguards that remain

- **Nobody can pay or reject their own request.** Someone else who holds the Record permission
  must act on it. This also applies to administrators.
- **If a member edits a request while the treasurer has the payment form open,** the payment is
  stopped and the treasurer is asked to reload and review the change. Without this, a request
  could be changed from $20 to $200 between the treasurer opening it and paying it.
- A member can no longer edit or withdraw a request once it has been paid or rejected, and gets a
  clear message instead of a misleading one if that happens at the same moment.
- A receipt is still required, and reimbursements are still checked in bank reconciliation and
  appear in the monthly statements.

#### What the treasurer and board should know

- **Someone other than the treasurer must hold the Record permission.** At least two people need
  it so that the treasurer's own reimbursement requests can be paid. If only one person has it,
  the treasurer's own request cannot be paid by anyone. Confirm that a second person holds it, for
  example the site administrator.
- **The treasurer's account must hold the Record permission** so the request email links to a
  page where they can act. If it does not, the email goes to the other Record holders instead.
- **Reimbursements no longer use the approval threshold.** A $300 vendor check still waits for
  board approval; a $300 reimbursement does not. The only limit left on a single reimbursement is
  the $10,000 request ceiling, which was built as a guard against typos and is not a policy limit.
  There is also no cap on how many requests one member can submit. If the board wants a dollar
  ceiling above which approval returns, that is a decision for the board and not part of this
  release.
- Reimbursements are not yet summarized in the monthly report sent to the board, so the Paid tab
  is the board's review list for now.

#### Permissions

| Feature key | Required for |
|-------------|--------------|
| `ledger.record` | Paying or rejecting a reimbursement (the treasurer's review) |
| `ledger.view` | Viewing the Paid tab (board review) |
| `ledger.approve` | No longer used for reimbursements; still used for large vendor disbursements |

No new permission was added.

### Feature: Discard a reconciliation session you started by mistake

**Value:** Starting a reconciliation session against the wrong bank account used to leave no way
out in the product. The mistaken session also blocked starting the right one whenever their
statement periods overlapped, and the only fix was a hand edit to the database. The treasurer can
now discard it and start over.

#### What's New

- An open session now has a **Discard session** button at the top of its page, beside the Open
  badge.
- A confirmation says exactly what will be removed: the session, the statement lines uploaded to
  it, and the matches made against them.
- Nothing in the ledger changes. No transaction is edited, un-reconciled or deleted. Any
  transaction you created from a bank line while working in the session stays in your books. The
  confirmation says so, and if one landed on the wrong account, delete it from the transaction
  list.
- Right after discarding, you can start a new session for the same account and period.
- A closed session cannot be discarded directly. Reopen it first, which requires permission to
  manage the Ledger, then discard it. A session that has ever been reopened can only be discarded
  by someone who can manage the Ledger.
- Every discard is recorded in the Ledger's audit trail with who did it, when, and how many lines
  and matches were removed. The bank's own statement text is not copied into that record.
- The Treasury User's Guide's Bank Reconciliation section explains how to discard a session.

#### Permissions

| Feature key | Required for |
|-------------|--------------|
| `ledger.record` | Discarding an open session |
| `ledger.manage` | Additionally, discarding a session that was previously reopened |

No new permission was added.

### Feature: The Email Queue now keeps six months of history

**Value:** The Email Queue page used to keep every message the site had ever sent, including the
full text, the recipients, and any attached calendar file, indefinitely. It now keeps six months.
Older messages, sent or failed, are removed automatically the next time an administrator opens the
page, and the page says so. Nothing that still matters goes with them: dues reminders, event
announcements, board financial reports and donor acknowledgment letters each keep their own
permanent record of whether and when they went out.

#### What's Changed

- Messages older than six months are removed when an administrator opens the Email Queue page.
  Removal happens at that visit, so messages can linger past six months until someone next opens
  the page.
- Messages that are still being retried are never removed.
- The failed-email count in the sidebar stops counting messages nobody will ever retry. It may
  take one more page navigation to update on the visit that does the cleanup.
- An empty "Recently Sent" list now reads "No emails sent in the last 6 months" instead of
  implying nothing was ever sent.
- Nothing is removed on the day this ships. The oldest messages on record are from April 2026, so
  the first real cleanup happens on or after October 13, 2026.

### Fix: The "public fund holding undisbursed balance" warning now clears when the money turns over

**Value:** The Ledger used to warn on any Activity, Charitable or Scholarship fund whose first-ever
income was more than a year old and that still had a balance. For a working fund that never drains
to zero, the warning could never go away, which taught everyone to ignore the compliance panel.

#### What's Changed

- The warning now looks at how long the money has been sitting there. Money is aged oldest-first:
  spending is counted against the oldest dollars on hand, so income and the opening balance are
  used up in the order they arrived. The warning shows how many dollars of each fund's balance are
  past the holding period, for example "$2,140.00 of its $8,973.75 balance has been on hand more
  than 365 days."
- Ordinary activity clears it; disbursing or sweeping the remainder clears the rest.
- The Ledger settings page and the Treasury User's Guide now describe the rule the same way.

#### What the treasurer should know

- **A public fund that holds only an opening balance and has had no activity will now show the
  warning.** The opening balance has no date, so it is treated as the oldest money in the fund, and
  that money really is old. Expect this on any dormant fund that was set up with a starting
  balance. Disbursing it or recording it as swept clears the warning.
- **Please confirm that the oldest-first reading matches how the club thinks about this rule.** It
  is recorded as a decision (DECISION-105) and the treasurer's sign-off on it is requested.
- The warning is advisory. Recording or redating income inside the window, or moving an old balance
  to another fund, makes money look newer than it is. The reconciliation locks and the audit log
  remain the controls against that.
