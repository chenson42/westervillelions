## 1.67.0 — 2026-08-12

### Feature: Say what a gift was for

**Value:** The first letters going out are Rudolph Run sponsorships, and a receipt that doesn't say
what it was for makes a sponsor work to remember why they gave.

#### What's New

- **An optional "what this gift was for"** on each acknowledgment, in your own words. It reads as a
  natural clause in the letter — *"…received a cash contribution of $500.00 from you in support of
  the 2026 Rudolph Run."* Leave it blank and the letter is exactly as it was before.
- **It's yours to write, not the bookkeeping label.** The ledger category behind a gift is an
  accounting name; the donor sees the phrase you type.
- **It can be corrected until the letter is sent.** After that it's locked — a sent letter's wording
  is a record of what the donor was actually told, not something to revise afterwards.
- **Changing it clears an already-drafted letter**, so a letter can never be emailed with wording the
  record no longer agrees with. You're told to generate it again.
- It also appears on quid-pro-quo letters, alongside — never instead of — the required disclosure.

---

### Enhancement: The letters look and sign like the club's

**Changes:**
- **The club logo now heads every letter**, printed or emailed. This was noted as missing when letter
  generation first shipped.
- **Letters are signed by whoever currently holds the office of Treasurer**, taken from the board.
  Until now the signature name was a hand-typed setting that shipped empty, so letters went out
  signed with a title and no name at all. Type a name yourself and yours still wins.
- If the office is briefly unset, the letter still goes out with the title alone rather than being
  withheld — a donor is entitled to their receipt.

---

### Enhancement: A postal address is no longer required to produce a letter

**Value:** The club was withholding a legally valid receipt over a field that never appeared on it.

**Changes:**
- A donor with no mailing address on file **can now be sent a letter**. The IRS requirements for a
  written acknowledgment — the club's name, the amount, and the goods-or-services statement — do not
  include the donor's address, and the letter never printed it. (Contributor addresses belong to a
  different form filed with the IRS, not to the donor's receipt.)
- A missing address is now shown as a note rather than a blocker, since it still tells you the letter
  has to be emailed or handed over instead of posted.

---

### Defect Fix: Three ways a donor could not be linked to a gift

**Problem:** The treasurer reported being unable to link a donor to a gift that had already been
acknowledged.

**Root Cause:** Three separate faults, each hiding the next.

1. Once an acknowledgment was recorded, the queue offered only *Generate Letter* and *Mark Sent*.
   "No donor linked" was plain text with nothing to click, and the only donor control had been inside
   the dialog that disappears the moment the acknowledgment exists. Acknowledge first, link second,
   and there was no way back.
2. Linking a donor updated the transaction but **not** the acknowledgment. Since the letter reads the
   acknowledgment's copy, a gift could show the donor's name on screen and still produce a letter
   addressed to nobody.
3. Sent acknowledgments had no screen at all, so anything already marked sent vanished from the only
   place a donor would naturally be attached.

**Fix:**
- Donor linking is now always available in the acknowledgment queue, whatever state the row is in.
- Linking a donor updates the gift and its acknowledgment together, in one database transaction, so
  the two can never disagree.
- A **Sent Acknowledgments** view lists what has already gone out, shows whether each was emailed or
  printed, flags any with no donor attached, and allows linking one.

**Testing:**
- [x] Link a donor to an already-acknowledged gift, then generate the letter and confirm the donor's
      name appears
- [x] Link a donor from the sent view and confirm the missing-donor count falls
- [x] Confirm a letter still generates for a donor with no postal address
- [x] Confirm a blank gift purpose reproduces the previous letter word for word
