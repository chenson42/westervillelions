## 1.64.0 — 2026-08-09

### Feature: Project or Activity Proposals

**Value:** Members have proposed projects on a paper form that ended with "give the completed form to
any Board Member." Once handed over, the proposer had no way of knowing whether it was read,
discussed, or decided. This puts the form in the member portal, notifies the board automatically, and
— the part the paper form never had — tells the proposer what happened.

The board's Communication Policy, approved at the August 7 retreat, requires this form be submitted
with any publicity request, so it needs to be usable club-wide.

#### What's New

- **A much shorter form.** The paper version asked eighteen questions, most of them open essay boxes.
  The online one asks **five required questions and about five optional ones**, in three short
  sections. It was cut deliberately, against research on why people abandon long forms — and against
  what comparable clubs actually ask for.
- **Your details are filled in already** — name, email, phone, and the date. You answer questions
  about the project, not about yourself.
- **"I don't know yet" is a real answer.** On money, dates, and how many volunteers you'd need, not
  knowing is a proper answer rather than a blank the board has to interpret. An honest "not sure" is
  more useful to them than an invented number.
- **You don't have to finish in one sitting.** The form saves as you type, and you can leave and come
  back. Nothing is sent to anyone until you submit.
- **A chairperson isn't required to submit.** The paper form said the board wouldn't consider a
  proposal without one. Online you can say "not yet identified" and still put the idea forward — the
  board sees that no chair is named and can help find one, rather than the idea never surfacing.
- **The money question adapts.** You're asked one thing — does this need money from the club up front?
  The cost and income questions only appear if they apply.
- **The board is emailed the moment you submit**, with the proposal summarized in the message.
- **You're emailed too** — a confirmation when you submit, and again every time the board's decision
  changes. You don't have to remember to check.
- **You can follow where it stands.** Submitted, under review, approved, declined, or deferred, with
  the board's own note and, once minutes are written, a link to the meeting that decided it.
- **The board gets a proper review screen** rather than a proposal buried in an inbox — with the
  decision history kept permanently, so a project deferred twice and then approved shows all three
  steps rather than only the last.
- **Proposals are private to their proposer and the board**, so a half-formed idea isn't on display
  club-wide.

#### Permissions

| Feature key | Required for |
|-------------|--------------|
| `proposals.review` | Seeing all proposals and recording board decisions |

Held by administrators and board members. **Any member can submit a proposal and follow their own** —
no permission needed.

#### New Routes

- `GET /members/proposals` — your proposals, and the form
- `GET /admin/proposals` — the board's review screen

#### Note

An approved proposal stays a proposal. It does not automatically become an event, a campaign, or a
budget line — someone still sets those up deliberately, as they do today.
