Two words, and what's behind them

We Serve

Lions Clubs International began in Chicago in 1917, when a young insurance man named Melvin Jones asked a simple question: what if business clubs did something beyond business? Our own club has served Westerville since 1928, which is very nearly a hundred years.

In 1925, at Cedar Point here in Ohio, Helen Keller stood in front of the Lions convention and asked them to become “Knights of the Blind in my crusade against darkness.” They said yes. A century later it is still why Lions everywhere work on sight.

1.4 million

Lions worldwide, in about 48,000 clubs across more than 200 countries.

Locally decided

Our members choose which causes we fund. Nobody hands us a list.

All volunteer

No paid staff. Every dollar is moved by someone in this room.

The wider Lions

The eight global causes

Lions International organizes its work worldwide around eight causes. We report our service against them, which is how an afternoon's work in Westerville becomes part of a global total.

Vision

Preventing avoidable blindness, and supporting people who are blind.

Hunger

Access to nutritious food.

Environment

Healthier, more sustainable communities.

Childhood Cancer

Support for children and their families.

Diabetes

Prevention, and better living with it.

Disaster Relief

Help when disaster strikes, and after.

Humanitarian

The world's most crucial needs.

Youth

Young people making positive choices, the next generation of service.

Originally five causes; expanded to eight over time. Diabetes was added in 2017, at the Lions centennial.

How the club works

The rhythm of a month

1st Thursday · 7:00 PM

General Meeting. A speaker or program, plus club business. The one to come to if you only come to one.

3rd Thursday · 7:00 PM

Activities Meeting. Planning the hands-on work, who is doing what, and when. Where projects get organized.

Monthly · Tuesday

Board Meeting. Decisions on spending and direction. Open to every member, you are welcome to attend.

New this year. We used to hold two club meetings a month. From September, the second one becomes the Activities Meeting, same night of the month, different purpose: less business, more planning the work.

Where: Every meeting is at The Landings of Westerville, 1st Floor Training Room, same room each time.

At a meeting

What to expect

  • Wear a name badge. We put them out at every meeting, so there is nothing to remember and nothing to forget at home. Pick yours up on the way in. It is the simplest courtesy we extend to the newest person in the room, and to the guest who doesn't know anyone yet.
  • Come a few minutes early. The conversation before things get started is where you'll actually get to know people.
  • Tail twisting. A light-hearted raffle that raises real money for the club. Bring some cash.
  • Bring a guest. Anyone curious is welcome at a General Meeting. Most of us joined because somebody invited us.
  • Record your service hours. Sheets are on the table at every meeting.
  • You can just listen. Nobody will put you on the spot in your first month.

Worth knowing

We are deliberately spending down the Club fund

The Club's operating fund has built up more money than it needs. Sitting on member dues is not the point of a service club, so the board has planned this year to put that surplus to work rather than let it accumulate.

$5,000 for the Fouse/McVay Cabin

Earmarked for the educational center. We expect the project won't be ready this year, so the money is tracked and carried rather than quietly reabsorbed.

The rest

Marketing, program supplies, member recognition and hospitality. The things that make the club work, and make people want to come.

So if you see the Club budget planned to spend more than it takes in this year: that is on purpose, and it is the responsible thing to do with a surplus.

Worth understanding

We are two organizations

This trips up nearly everybody, and it matters when you write a check.

The Club

Runs the club itself: dues, meetings, insurance, and our dues to Lions International. Funded by members. Payments to the Club are not tax-deductible.

The Foundation

A tax-exempt 501(c)(3) charitable organization, EIN 32-0467239. Everything raised from the public and everything given away runs through it. Gifts to the Foundation are tax-deductible.

Rule of thumb: money from us is Club; money from the public, and money going out to causes, is Foundation. Lions International is built the same way. LCI and its charitable arm, LCIF, are separate for exactly this reason.

Where you fit

There's a committee with your name on it

There is a place for everyone in this club, and you do not need to be an expert at anything. A committee is a handful of people who care about the same thing, sitting down together to make something happen, offering ideas, giving time, and changing something they wanted changed anyway.

How it works

  1. Lead or join a committee.
  2. Meet, and share ideas.
  3. Develop a plan together.
  4. Present it to the board for approval.
  5. Put your plan into action.

Four people is the target

A chair and three other Lions is enough to share the work, and small enough that everyone's ideas still matter. More are welcome; fewer still counts.

Step 4 is easier than it sounds

Presenting to the board is the proposal form on the portal. About ten questions, and the board is emailed the moment you send it.

Please do this one thing

Record your service hours

Why it matters

  • It is how Westerville's work is counted in the global total that Lions report each year. None of it counts unless someone writes it down.
  • It is evidence when we apply for grants, and when we ask a business to sponsor us.
  • A high share of clubs reporting is how a district is judged healthy.
Lions International puts it bluntly: “If it's not reported, it's not done.”

What counts

  • Time at a project, and the driving, shopping and preparing around it.
  • Committee work and planning time.
  • Service you do on your own that furthers the Lions causes.

Two sheets come with this packet: one to keep at home, one you'll find on the table at every meeting.

Your member portal

If it doesn't work, or you'd rather it didn't

If you're stuck

  • Ask at a meeting. Find Chris Henson or anyone on the Technology Committee. It is almost always a five-second fix, and you will not be the first to ask.
  • Can't sign in? Use Forgot password first, that solves most of it.
  • Wrong details showing? You can correct your own address, phone and photo from your profile.

If you'd rather have paper

  • That's completely fine. Nothing in this club requires you to use a computer, and nobody will chase you about it.
  • The directory and the calendar both print, ask and you'll be handed a copy.
  • Ask the Secretary for printed minutes, or the Treasurer for a printed financial statement.
  • Tell any board member you'd prefer to be reached by phone or post, and that's how you'll be reached.
The portal exists to save you effort, not to become another thing you have to manage.

Your idea counts

Have a project in mind? Propose it.

Any member can put a project or fundraiser in front of the board. It is a short form on the portal, about ten questions, and “I don't know yet” is a perfectly good answer to most of them.

You submit

Fill it in over a few sittings if you like, it saves as you go.

The board sees it

Emailed the moment you send it; taken up at their next meeting.

You hear back

Emailed at every step, and you can see exactly where it stands.

You don't need a chairperson lined up, and you don't need to know the budget. Bring the idea; we'll help with the rest. Prefer paper? Hand a written note to any board member, it goes to the same place.
