# UX/Conversion Audit — Westerville Lions Club
**Date:** 2026-04-06
**Scope:** https://westervillelions.vercel.app (all public pages)
**Method:** Two-pass review — Designer/Strategist + First-Time End User simulation

---

## Pass 1: Designer & Strategist Verdict

The site is clean and structurally sound. The design system is consistent. The problems are almost entirely **copy and conversion architecture** — where you send people, what you ask them to do, and what you say to get them to do it. You're treating this like a brochure when it should be a recruiting machine.

## Pass 2: First-Time Visitor Experience

> *I landed on the homepage. Nice photo. I read "Creating and fostering a spirit of understanding among all people for humanitarian needs by providing voluntary services through community involvement." I nodded politely and immediately forgot every word. I clicked "Learn More" expecting to get a feel for what this club does. I landed on the About page, read some history, scrolled past board members, and left. I never clicked Join. Nobody told me I should.*

---

## Critical

These are conversion killers. Fix these first.

---

### 1. Hero body copy is LCI boilerplate — nobody reads it, nobody remembers it

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:153–157`

The full mission statement verbatim ("Creating and fostering a spirit of understanding among all people for humanitarian needs...") is dry 1917-era organizational language. It reads like a legal filing. First-time visitors want to know what you *actually do in Westerville*, not what Lions International stands for globally.

**Fix:** Replace with 2–3 punchy, concrete, local sentences:
```
We feed families at the Westerville Food Pantry.
We fund high school scholarships.
We clean up Hoover Reservoir.
That's Lions.
```
Lead with action and place. Cut the mission statement to the Mission page where it belongs.

---

### 2. "Support Our Mission" CTA links to `/campaigns`, not `/donate`

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:295`

The bottom homepage CTA button — the last conversion opportunity before a visitor bounces — links to `/campaigns`. That route is not in the public nav and likely requires auth or 404s publicly. This is a dead CTA on your highest-intent exit section.

**Fix:** Change `href="/campaigns"` to `href="/donate"`.

---

### 3. Join form has 13+ fields including DOB, Gender, Spouse Name — before they even know you

**File:** `src/app/join/page.tsx`

Fields requested: First Name, MI, Last Name, Suffix, Gender, Date of Birth, Occupation, Spouse's Name, Street Address, City, State, ZIP, Phone, Email, Membership Type, Member Sponsor. That's a background-check-level ask from someone who just discovered your club.

Then: *"Application acceptance requires board approval and acknowledgment of Lions Clubs International membership standards."* This sounds like you can be rejected. It's off-putting.

**Fix — two steps:**
1. Slim the public form to: First Name, Last Name, Email, Phone, Membership Type, and an optional "How did you hear about us?"
2. Reframe the disclaimer: "We welcome all applicants. The board meets monthly to formally welcome new members into the club."
Collect full member data (DOB, address, gender, etc.) after acceptance via the member portal onboarding.

---

### 4. "47 Active Members" is a liability, not an asset

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:186`

This stat is pulled live from the database. 47 members is honest, but for a civic club trying to recruit, it signals "small, possibly struggling." Prospects don't think "I want to be member 48" — they think "Is this club dying?"

**Fix:** Replace the middle stat with an impact metric that can't be questioned:
- "20+ Events Per Year"
- "500+ Volunteer Hours"
- "Since 1928" (use the founding year twice if needed, it's legitimizing)
- Or simply: "Lifelong Friendships" — aspirational, not falsifiable.

---

### 5. Zero social proof anywhere on the site

There are no member testimonials, no quotes, no "here's why I joined" stories anywhere — including the Join page, which is where a prospect goes when they're considering membership. The About page has a list of board members with tenure, which is a start, but it's not the same as a human voice.

Social proof is the #1 conversion driver for a membership organization. People join clubs because people like them are in the club.

**Fix:** Add 2–3 short member quotes to the Join page. Even first name + tenure is enough:
> *"I've been a Lion for 17 years. The friendships I've made here are unlike anything else." — Debbie B., member since 2009*

---

## High Impact

These have significant effect on clarity, trust, or bounce rate.

---

### 6. "Connect" in the nav is ambiguous — nobody knows it means Contact

**File:** `src/components/layout/` (nav component)

"Connect" is the page title for what is functionally your contact page. Visitors looking for a phone number or email address scan navs for "Contact." "Connect" sounds like social media. This creates micro-friction at every page load.

**Fix:** Rename to "Contact" or "Contact Us."

---

### 7. "Learn More" sends visitors to the About page — a dead end

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:166–171`

The secondary hero CTA sends curious visitors to `/about`. The About page is informational — it has history, board members, and meeting times. It doesn't push visitors back to Join or to attend a meeting. Visitors who click "Learn More" are warm leads; the About page lets them go cold.

**Fix:** Change the secondary CTA to "Attend a Meeting" and link to `/contact` (which shows meeting times and location). Alternatively, add a prominent "Come to Our Next Meeting" section at the top of the About page.

---

### 8. "Stay Connected" social section interrupts homepage content flow

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:233–282`

The social media link block appears **between** the Service Cards and the Featured Events/Announcements section. This is a jarring interrupt. A first-time visitor who just absorbed the service areas should next see "here's when and how to get involved" — not a list of social media platforms.

**Fix:** Move the social links to the footer (already there) or to the very bottom of the homepage. Bring Featured Events immediately after Service Cards. Let the "Stay Connected" block precede the bottom CTA, not interrupt the main content.

---

### 9. Donate page: "Where Your Donation Goes" has zero impact specificity

**File:** `src/app/donate/page.tsx:186–199`

Four gray cards with category names and single sentences. There are no dollar amounts, no real outcomes, no before-and-after. "Scholarships, educational programs, and youth leadership development" — how much? How many students? Per year?

Additionally, these cards use `bg-gray-50 rounded-lg` which breaks the established design system (should be `rounded-2xl`).

**Fix:** Add one concrete impact line per card:
```
Youth Programs: "$500 scholarships awarded to Westerville students each year"
Hunger Relief: "Partnering with Mid-Ohio Food Bank to serve hundreds of local families"
```

---

### 10. About page: meeting time/location is buried mid-page

**File:** `src/app/about/page.tsx`

The meeting schedule ("1st and 3rd Thursday, 6:30 PM, The Landings") and "All meetings are open to visitors" are the most actionable pieces of information for a curious prospect. They appear after the club history and sometimes after the board roster depending on scroll position.

**Fix:** Surface meeting info to the first or second section — ideally immediately after the hero, as a standalone "Come Visit Us" card. This is your single best conversion tool for borderline prospects.

---

### 11. "Member Login" in primary nav competes with recruitment

**File:** Nav component

Member Login gets a primary nav slot alongside Mission, Events, and Donate. Existing members know where to log in; you don't need to advertise it to prospects. The nav should be 100% focused on converting visitors into members or donors.

**Fix:** Move "Member Login" to a small ghost button or icon on the far right of the nav bar, visually separate from the content links.

---

### 12. Events page signals no activity frequency

**File:** `src/app/events/page.tsx`

A first-time visitor sees a list of upcoming events but has no sense of how often this club meets or serves. If bi-monthly meetings aren't listed as recurring events, the page may look sparse. Regularity of activity is the strongest "this club is alive and healthy" signal.

**Fix:** Ensure bi-monthly club meetings appear as public recurring events. Even just "Monthly Club Meeting — 1st and 3rd Thursday, The Landings" as recurring events dramatically improves the density signal.

---

## Nice to Have

Low effort or low impact, but worth noting.

---

### 13. "100% Community Focused" is meaningless

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:189–191`

This is not a real metric. Every nonprofit could claim it. Nobody reads this and thinks "Oh, 100%! Most clubs are only 87% community focused."

**Fix:** Replace with a real number your club can back up — events per year, volunteer hours, scholarship dollars awarded, years at the same location, etc.

---

### 14. Service card copy is generic — no local specificity

**File:** `src/app/page.tsx:206–228`

"Supporting education, scholarships, and youth activities to empower the next generation" could be the copy for any Lions club in the country. Add one Westerville-specific detail to each card (school name, event name, food bank name).

---

### 15. Footer newsletter signup only reachable from Contact page

Newsletter subscription is only on `/contact`. Most visitors won't find it there. A small "Stay in the Loop" email capture in the footer sitewide would passively grow your list from visitors who aren't ready to join.

---

### 16. Contact form "Subject" field is unnecessary friction

**File:** `src/app/contact/page.tsx`

You'll respond to every message regardless of subject. The field adds a cognitive step with no benefit. Remove it.

---

### 17. Donate page "Thank You" block uses `bg-blue-50` not brand tokens

**File:** `src/app/donate/page.tsx:142`

`bg-blue-50` is a hardcoded Tailwind blue that doesn't match `lions-blue`. Minor design inconsistency.

**Fix:** Change to `bg-lions-blue/5` or `bg-blue-50` → consistent with other info panels.

---

### 18. Mission page: 8 emoji service areas reads like a SaaS features grid

**File:** `src/app/mission/page.tsx:137–151`

Eight small cards with emoji icons and single-paragraph descriptions is a format borrowed from software landing pages. For a service organization, this undersells the human impact. Nobody joins a club because they saw 8 emoji tiles.

**Fix (if bandwidth allows):** Collapse to 4 service areas with photos and a single impact metric each. Depth over breadth.
